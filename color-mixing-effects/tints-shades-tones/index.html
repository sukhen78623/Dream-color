Free tints, shades and tones generator

A tint is a color mixed with white, a shade is a color mixed with black, and a tone is a color mixed with grey. Together they give you softer, deeper and more muted versions of one color that always belong together, which makes them ideal for buttons, backgrounds, borders and charts.

This tool blends in OKLab by default, so the steps look evenly spaced to the eye and keep their hue instead of drifting. It also builds a 50–950 scale like the ones used by Tailwind and design systems, and shows a contrast ratio on every swatch so you can see which color pairs are safe for text.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between tints, shades and tones?

Tints add white and get lighter, shades add black and get darker, and tones add grey and get more muted. The hue stays the same in all three.

How do I make a lighter or darker version of a color?

Pick your color, then tap a swatch in the Tints row for lighter versions or in the Shades row for darker ones. Use the range slider to control how close to white or black the last step gets.

Why use OKLab instead of plain sRGB?

OKLab matches how people see lightness, so each step changes by a similar visible amount and colors stay vivid. Plain sRGB mixing can look uneven or grey in the middle. You can switch to sRGB if you want the classic result.

What is the 50–950 color scale?

It is a set of eleven steps from very light (50) to very dark (950), with your color placed at the closest step. Use light steps for backgrounds, middle steps for buttons and dark steps for text. Copy it straight into Tailwind, CSS variables or SCSS.

What do AAA, AA, Large and Low mean on the swatches?

They show the WCAG contrast of the swatch text against its background. AAA is 7 or higher, AA is 4.5 or higher for normal text, Large is 3 or higher for big text only, and Low means the pair is hard to read.
