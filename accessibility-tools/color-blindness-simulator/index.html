Free color blindness simulator

Color blindness, or color vision deficiency, changes how people tell colors apart. A chart, a map or a button that looks clear to you can become confusing for someone who sees red and green as similar shades. This simulator shows your image or palette through the main types of color vision deficiency so you can find problems before your users do.

Use the severity slider to move from full color blindness (like protanopia) to mild forms (like protanomaly), and drag the comparison bar to see the original and simulated image side by side.

Types of color blindness you can test

  1. Protanopia and protanomaly: reds look darker and are confused with greens and browns.
  2. Deuteranopia and deuteranomaly: greens are confused with reds. This is the most common type.
  3. Tritanopia and tritanomaly: blues and yellows are hard to tell apart. This is rare.
  4. Achromatopsia: very little or no color is seen. This is very rare.

How to use the simulator

  1. Upload an image, or try the demo image.
  2. Pick a condition and adjust the severity.
  3. Drag the bar to compare, or scroll to see every condition at once.
  4. Paste your brand or chart colors into the palette test to find pairs that look alike.

Frequently asked questions

What is color blindness?

Color blindness is the common name for color vision deficiency, where some colors are hard to tell apart. It is usually inherited and most often affects red and green. It does not mean a person sees only black and white.

How many people are color blind?

Roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some form of red-green color vision deficiency, though rates vary between populations. Blue-yellow and complete color blindness are much rarer.

How accurate is a color blindness simulator?

It is a good approximation based on published color vision models, but real perception differs from person to person, and screens and lighting also matter. Treat it as a design check, not a medical test.

How do I make colors color blind friendly?

Do not rely on color alone. Add labels, icons, patterns or different shapes, keep strong lightness contrast between neighboring colors, and test your palette here and with a contrast checker.

What is the difference between protanopia and protanomaly?

The ending -opia means a type of cone cell is missing or not working, so the effect is strong. The ending -anomaly means it works partly, so the effect is milder. The severity slider moves between the two.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is processed in your browser and never leaves your device.
