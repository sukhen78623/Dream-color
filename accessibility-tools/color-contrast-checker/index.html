Free WCAG color contrast checker

Contrast is the difference in brightness between text and its background. Low contrast makes content hard to read for people with low vision, color blindness, or anyone on a phone in bright sunlight. This checker calculates the exact WCAG contrast ratio of any two colors and tells you whether the pair passes level AA and level AAA.

It also shows the APCA Lc score, a newer method proposed for WCAG 3, and suggests the nearest accessible colors when your pair fails, so you can keep your brand color and still meet the standard.

WCAG contrast requirements

  1. Normal text: 4.5:1 for AA, 7:1 for AAA.
  2. Large text (24px regular or 18.66px bold and bigger): 3:1 for AA, 4.5:1 for AAA.
  3. User interface parts and graphics such as buttons, form borders and icons: 3:1 for AA.

How to use the contrast checker

  1. Choose or type your text color and background color.
  2. Read the ratio and the pass or fail result for each level.
  3. Check the live preview, and edit its text to try your own wording.
  4. If a pair fails, tap one of the suggested colors, then copy the result or the link.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good color contrast ratio?

For normal body text aim for at least 4.5:1, which is WCAG AA. A ratio of 7:1 or more meets AAA and is more comfortable for many readers. The maximum possible ratio is 21:1, black on white.

What is the difference between WCAG AA and AAA?

AA is the common legal and practical target and needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. AAA is stricter and needs 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text.

What counts as large text in WCAG?

Large text is at least 18 point (about 24px) in regular weight, or at least 14 point (about 18.66px) in bold.

Do buttons and icons need contrast too?

Yes. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for at least 3:1 contrast for the visual parts of controls, such as input borders and button outlines, and for meaningful graphics like icons. Disabled controls and pure decoration are exempt.

Did WCAG 2.2 change the contrast ratios?

No. WCAG 2.2 keeps the same contrast thresholds as WCAG 2.1: 4.5:1, 3:1 and 7:1 for text, and 3:1 for non-text parts.

What is APCA?

APCA is a newer contrast method proposed for WCAG 3. It gives a lightness contrast score called Lc and takes text polarity into account, so dark on light and light on dark can score differently. It is still a draft, so use it as an extra guide beside the WCAG 2 ratio.

Are my colors saved anywhere?

No. All calculations happen in your browser and nothing is sent to a server.
