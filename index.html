      Color Guide: Using These Tools Well

      A short, practical background on palettes, gradients, color formats and accessibility, so the numbers on screen turn into good design decisions.

      How to build a balanced palette

      Start with one color you are sure about, usually the one that carries your brand or mood. Lock it, then let the generator suggest the rest. A dependable proportion is the 60-30-10 rule: about 60% a calm dominant color, 30% a supporting color and 10% a bold accent.

      Keep at least one very light and one very dark color in the set. They give you backgrounds and text, and they make the saturated colors look intentional instead of loud.

      Color harmony modes explained

      • Random ignores relationships entirely. Use it to break out of a rut.
      • Complementary pairs opposite hues. It is high contrast, so use one side as the accent.
      • Analogous uses neighbors on the wheel. It feels calm and is hard to get wrong.
      • Triadic spaces three hues evenly. It is lively; let one hue dominate.
      • Monochromatic varies only lightness and saturation of one hue, for a unified look.

      CSS gradients explained

      A linear gradient blends colors along a line at an angle you choose; 0deg points up and 90deg points right. A radial gradient spreads outward from a point as a circle or ellipse. Each color has a stop position, and moving stops closer together makes a sharper transition.

      For a gradient that fades out, keep the same color on both stops and lower the opacity of the last one, rather than fading to the word transparent. For page backgrounds, include a solid fallback color and background-size: cover, as the Background mode does.

      HEX, RGB and HSL: which to use

      HEX (#5A4AF0) is short and universal, so it is the usual choice for design handoff. RGB (rgb(90, 74, 240)) matches how screens mix light and is convenient when scripting or adding opacity. HSL (hsl(246, 85%, 60%)) is the easiest to reason about: raise lightness for a tint, lower it for a shade, reduce saturation for a muted tone.

      All three describe the same sRGB colors, so converting between them loses nothing apart from rounding to whole numbers.

      Contrast and accessibility

      Contrast ratio compares the lightness of text and its background, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white). WCAG level AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, which means roughly 24px regular or 18.7px bold and above. Level AAA raises those to 7:1 and 4.5:1.

      Interface parts such as input borders, icons and chart lines need 3:1 against their surroundings. A passing ratio is a strong start, but it is not a full accessibility review: font size, weight and context still matter.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is Dream Color really free, and do I need an account?

      Yes. Every tool is free, there is no sign-up or login, and there is no limit on how many palettes or gradients you can generate.

      Are the colors I pick stored or sent anywhere?

      No. All calculations, copying and downloads happen in your browser. Dream Color has no backend or database, and it does not collect or transmit the colors you choose or generate.

      How do I lock a color in the palette generator?

      Select the lock icon on a color. Locked colors stay in place when you press the space bar or the Generate button, and the first locked color sets the base hue for the harmony modes. Picking a color manually locks it automatically.

      Can I use the generated palettes in commercial projects?

      Individual colors and simple color combinations are generally not protected by copyright, so you can use palettes you generate in your own work. If you are imitating a specific brand identity, check trademark rules. This is general information, not legal advice.

      What is the difference between HEX, RGB and HSL?

      They describe the same colors in different ways. HEX is a compact six-digit code, RGB gives the amount of red, green and blue from 0 to 255, and HSL describes hue, saturation and lightness, which makes it easiest to adjust a color by hand.

      What contrast ratio do I need for accessible text?

      WCAG level AA asks for at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Level AAA asks for 7:1 and 4.5:1. User interface components and graphics need at least 3:1.

      How do I make a gradient fade to transparent?

      Open the Transparent tab, keep the same color on both stops and set the opacity of the last stop to 0. The CSS uses rgba() values so the fade stays clean, and the PNG download keeps the transparency.
